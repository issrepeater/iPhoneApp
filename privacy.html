Privacy Policy

ISS Repeater for iOS · Last updated 9 September 2026

The short version: ISS Repeater collects nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no server that belongs to this app. Everything you type stays on your phone.

Information the app does not collect

ISS Repeater does not collect, transmit, sell, or share personal information. It contains no analytics or advertising SDKs, no crash-reporting service, and no user accounts. Nothing you enter is uploaded anywhere.

Location

The app uses your location in two places, both optional and both on the device only:

  • STATION 01's coordinates. If you turn on "Use current location," the app asks iOS for a single location fix and shows the resulting latitude and longitude in the coordinate field. That value is used on the device to compute pass geometry.
  • The sky finder (LIVE VIEW). While the sky finder is open, the app reads your location continuously so the ISS's position in the sky and the magnetic-declination correction are computed from where you are actually standing. Updates stop the moment you leave that screen. If location is off, the finder points from STATION 01's typed coordinates instead.

Your location is never transmitted off the device and is not stored beyond the coordinate field described above. You can decline or revoke location permission at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services and simply type coordinates instead; every feature still works.

Camera

The sky finder shows the rear camera's live view so the ISS can be marked on the real sky. The preview is displayed on screen and nothing else: no photo or video is captured, saved, analysed, or transmitted, and no image data ever leaves the camera pipeline. If you decline camera access the finder still works — the bearings, the horizon and the ISS marker are all computed and are drawn over a blank panel instead of the camera image.

Motion sensors

The sky finder reads the phone's gyroscope, accelerometer and magnetometer to know which way the camera is pointing. Those readings are used on the device for the current frame and are not recorded.

Data stored on your device

Stored locally, in the app's own storage, and never sent anywhere:

  • The station coordinates you entered or picked.
  • Your settings — station mode, search span, time zone, minimum elevation, units, range limits, night mode, notification preference, and any manually entered element set.
  • The passes you have saved, with the coordinates and settings they were computed from.
  • A cached copy of the ISS orbital element set.

Deleting the app removes all of it.

Network requests

The app makes two kinds of outbound request, neither of which carries personal data:

  • Orbital elements. Fetching current two-line elements for the ISS from CelesTrak, with AMSAT as a fallback. These are public, read-only feeds. The request sends no identifiers — it does not include your coordinates, your settings, or anything about you.
  • Map tiles. When you open SELECT FROM MAP, Apple Maps (MapKit, built into iOS) loads map imagery from Apple for the region shown on screen. That is governed by Apple's own privacy policy; the app itself sends Apple nothing beyond what MapKit needs to draw the map.

As with any request to any website, the operators of those services can see the IP address the request came from. Those services are operated independently of this app and are governed by their own privacy practices.

Notifications

If you enable notifications, reminders are scheduled locally by iOS on your device from the passes on your saved list. There is no push server and no notification token is sent anywhere.

Children

ISS Repeater is a utility for licensed amateur radio operators. It is not directed at children and knowingly collects no information from anyone, of any age.

Third-party code and data

The app links SatelliteKit (MIT licence) for orbital propagation. It is compiled into the app, performs no network activity of its own, and collects nothing. Magnetic declination is computed on the device from the World Magnetic Model published by NOAA/NCEI and the British Geological Survey; the model is a table of numbers bundled with the app and involves no network access.
